# Accuracy against published benchmarks

Generated by `npm run accuracy` (tools/accuracy.mjs) from data/benchmarks.json, data/calibration.json, data/machines.json, data/bench-hardware.json and data/ref-models.json. Do not edit by hand: change the data or the model and run it again.

Every published row the cost model can represent is estimated with the factors the presets use (fitted per hardware family by `npm run fit`, tools/fit-families.mjs) and compared with the published number. 185 measurements from 95 rows are used, on 42 machines; 36 more are older builds of the same measurement, and 116 are left out with a reason (at the end). The reference ThinkPad presets are calibrated on their own runs and never fitted, so they do not appear here.

Error is estimate / published − 1: positive means the model is too optimistic. "pp" is prompt processing (tokens/s over a prompt from an empty context, llama-bench pp512 or pp2048); "tg" is generation (tokens/s, tg128, tg32 or tg16).

## Where the model is weak

**Generation (tg) is the strong part; prompt processing (pp) is not.** Decode is close to bandwidth-bound and the model is a bandwidth model with a fixed cost per layer, so tg lands within 5.1% in the median (7.1% for a machine held out of its family's fit). Prefill depends on kernels, flash attention, the micro-batch and the build far more than on the spec peak, and one compute share per family misses individual machines by 9.1% in the median and 15% held out. Treat a time-to-first-token estimate as good to a factor of about 1.5, a tokens-per-second estimate as good to 10-20%.

**Mixtures of experts.** MoE prefill is off by 19% in the median against 8.3% for dense models: llama.cpp batches tokens per expert, so how much a prefill reaches depends on the expert count, the micro-batch and the kernel, which the model folds into one share per family (moeComputeEff). The MoE decode term (moeLayerMs) is fitted only where a family has both MoE and dense rows (Apple Metal (M1–M4), NVIDIA CUDA (GeForce, DGX Spark), NVIDIA CUDA (workstation, datacenter), AMD ROCm (Radeon, Ryzen AI), AMD Vulkan); elsewhere MoE models get no extra per-layer cost and the default prefill share, so expect them to be optimistic there. The gpt-oss files keep their experts at 4.25 bits and the rest wider; the model reads that split from the file size, but the attention and embedding width is an average.

**Datacenter parts.** Small models do not fill an H100, A100 or MI300X: llama.cpp reaches a far smaller share of their spec peak than on consumer cards, which is why NVIDIA and AMD ROCm are split into consumer and workstation/datacenter families. The datacenter fits rest on few machines (5 NVIDIA, 2 AMD Instinct), the H100 rows are left out because the source does not say SXM or PCIe, and the RTX PRO 6000's spec peak is itself doubtful (500 or 250 TFLOPS, data/machines.json). Large models on these parts are extrapolated.

**Thin families.** Intel (9 measurements on 3 machines), AMD Instinct (4 on 2) and CPU only (6 on 3, none of them a CPU preset: the 395's CPU, a 9900X and a 3950X with their own memory) are fitted on a handful of rows; the CPU presets (desktop, Threadripper, EPYC) are extrapolated from them, and the CPU per-layer cost (0.6263 ms) is large and uncertain. Apple M5 and later have no published FP16 peak, so their compute is fitted per GPU core on four machines.

**Builds and backends drift.** The same hardware gains up to a third on tg and two to four times on pp across llama.cpp builds: the M2 Ultra decodes Llama 2 7B Q4_0 +33% faster on the 2026 build than on the 2023 one (b008, b028). Most Apple M1-M4 rows come from that 2023 build, the only one the Metal thread has for them. The fit gives builds before 2025 a term of their own (pp × 0.8762, tg × 0.9192), but one term shared by the family does not reproduce the M2 Ultra's gain: its 2026 Q4_0 tg is -11%, so Apple decode estimates lean towards the older builds. AMD Vulkan prefill varies most with the weight format and build (a Qwen3 32B Q4_K_M pp512 on the R9700 is +300%); ROCm and Vulkan rows of the same AMD machine are fitted as separate families, and an AMD preset uses Vulkan's.

**What the model does not represent.** Sliding-window attention (Gemma 3, gpt-oss), multi-head latent attention (DeepSeek, GLM-4.7-Flash: left out), multi-GPU splits, partial offload, speculative decoding in the benchmarks, thermal limits of passively cooled laptops, and the host's effect on a card. The largest in-sample misses: r9700 qwen3-32b pp512 +300%; arcB580 llama2-7b pp512 +154%; r9700 qwen3-32b pp512 +91%; dgxSpark qwen2.5-7b pp2048 +82%; strixHalo gpt-oss-120b pp512 +63%; arcB580 llama2-7b pp512 -46%.

## Method

Least squares on the log of estimate / published over each family's usable rows (tools/bench.mjs): llama-bench pp (a prompt from an empty context) and tg (generation from an empty context), plain decoding, f16 KV, the row's micro-batch. Nelder-Mead in log space from a grid of starts, with a weak pull (weight 0.0002) towards {"bwEff":0.6,"computeEff":0.3,"layerMs":0.02,"moeLayerMs":0.02,"moeComputeEff":0.75} so a factor the rows cannot pin stays put. Of rows measuring the same thing only the newest build is kept (flash attention on, on a tie); builds before 2025-01-01 get a fitted build term (oldBuild) where the family also has newer rows. The reference ThinkPad is never fitted.

A row is used when its hardware is a preset or a bench-only machine (data/bench-hardware.json: V100, T4, MI100 and three CPUs that appear in clean rows), its model is a preset or a reference model built from its published configuration (data/ref-models.json, checked against the published parameter count), its bytes are known (the file size as printed, or the size of the same file in another row, or a preset weight format), it ran on llama.cpp at batch 1 with a backend of one of the families, and its notes do not make it another test. The estimate for a row is estimate() (src/parts/01e-estimate.ts) with the row's machine, model, bytes and micro-batch: pp as prompt tokens / prefill time, tg as tokens / decode time.
